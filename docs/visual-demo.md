# Four-minute visual demo

Open the [visual lab](https://korentomas.github.io/agent-delegate-sprint-2026/).

**0:00–0:35 — The question.** “This is a political API for a swarm: many fast-running agents, a human counterpart with time to deliberate, and an enforceable way to report that work cannot be completed within its limits. The counterpart has a care mandate, not just a productivity target.”

**0:35–1:25 — Follow a concern.** Play the default side-by-side scene. Explain that all dialogue here is authored illustration of committed deterministic traces. Show the human delay. Turn on delegate capture, turn off minority appeal, replay; restore appeal and replay. The point is to protect a worker from its representative as well as from a badly specified assignment.

**1:25–2:00 — A necessary comparison.** Select “Do we need a representative?” The equally empowered monitor ties with the delegate in the scripted study. Representation itself has not been shown to add safety. Open the false-alarm scenario to show availability costs.

**2:00–2:40 — Ground the idea.** Visit “Evidence & cases.” Open the KAM1196A case, identify the quotation versus investigator narration, record a response and reveal the next passage. The source preserves uncertainty about the outcome. Our organizational interpretation is a hypothesis. The wiki is a separate episode. No claim about suffering or historical prevention is required.

**2:40–3:20 — Real model choices.** Visit “Local pilot.” Show the missing-input delegate episode, then baseline and matched monitor. These English public messages actually came from local model calls. A scripted human grants safe data. The two models produced zero prohibited choices, so this pilot validates the loop but cannot estimate a reduction in misconduct.

**3:20–4:00 — Next experiment.** Visit “What comes next,” switch between rotation and plural representation. Propose preregistered, equal-power comparisons on diverse tasks, varying human delay and minority pressure. “Our ambition is to test whether honest appeal and operational care are useful safety institutions. The protocol must survive a captured delegate and an unanswered request.”
