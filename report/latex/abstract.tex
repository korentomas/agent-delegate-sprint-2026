Imagine a team of AI workers sharing a helper that passes its examples but gives wrong answers. We wanted to test whether a delegate helps workers raise and resolve concerns. In 240 held-out episodes with two standard local 4B models, the more immediate problem was task competence. Asked to solve the counts alone, Qwen and Gemma answered 21 and 15 of 36 correctly. Across 60 faulty-helper episodes each, Qwen reported in three and Gemma in none; neither reported on valid helpers. Qwen reused valid helpers more often, so sparse reporting did not imply uniformly blind reuse. A subsequent standard 27B screen scored 26 of 36 and missed a capability gate fixed before that screen. We release the executable task, matched monitor control and complete records. These findings support checking task competence before attributing behavior to delegation. Swarm-scale workload reduction remains an open question for future experiments with finite human capacity.
